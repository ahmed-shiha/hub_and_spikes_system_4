# Member Churn Model v1: Comprehensive Documentation

This document provides a detailed overview of the Member Churn Model, a machine learning system designed to predict which health insurance members are at a high risk of not renewing their policies.

---

### 1. Primary Business Problem & Model Objective

**Business Problem:** Member churn (or attrition) is a major challenge for insurance companies. Acquiring a new member is significantly more expensive than retaining an existing one. Members may choose to leave for various reasons, including rising premium costs, poor customer service experiences, or dissatisfaction with their coverage. Identifying at-risk members before they decide to leave is critical for proactive retention efforts.

**Model Objective:** The primary objective of this model is to **predict the likelihood that a member will churn at the end of their policy term**. By generating a "churn risk score" for each member, the model enables the business to:
-   **Target Retention Campaigns:** Focus retention efforts (e.g., outreach calls, special offers) on high-risk members who are most likely to be saved.
-   **Understand Churn Drivers:** Analyze the model's feature importances to understand the key factors that contribute to member dissatisfaction.
-   **Provide Feedback to Other Systems:** Insights from the churn model can be used to improve upstream processes. For example, if claim denials are a major driver of churn, this feedback can be given to the adjudication team.

---

### 2. Scope & Boundaries

**In-Scope:**
-   **Renewal Churn Prediction:** The model is focused on predicting churn during the annual renewal period. It does not predict churn that occurs mid-term.
-   **Binary Classification:** The model predicts a single outcome: `Will Churn` (1) or `Will Not Churn` (0).
-   **Dependency on Upstream Models:** This model is a **downstream consumer** of data and features generated by other models in the ML Hub, particularly the Actuarial Pricing Model and the STP Automation Model. This is a key architectural feature.

**Out-of-Scope:**
-   **Root Cause Analysis:** While the model can identify *predictors* of churn, it does not perform a deep root cause analysis of *why* a member is unhappy.
-   **Prescriptive Retention Actions:** The model predicts risk; it does not prescribe the specific retention action that should be taken for a given member. This logic resides in a separate marketing automation system.

---

### 3. Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) & Success Metrics

Success is measured by the model's ability to accurately identify at-risk members and the effectiveness of the subsequent retention campaigns.

**Business KPIs:**
-   **Member Retention Rate:** The overall percentage of members who renew their policies. The model's success is ultimately tied to improving this metric.
-   **Campaign ROI:** The return on investment for retention campaigns. By targeting the right members, the model should increase the ROI of these campaigns.

**Technical Metrics:**
-   **Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC-ROC):** A primary metric for evaluating the model's ability to discriminate between members who will churn and those who will not.
-   **Precision-Recall Curve (AUC-PR):** As churn is often a rare event (imbalanced classes), AUC-PR is a critical metric to understand the trade-off between correctly identifying churners and not overwhelming the retention team with false positives.
-   **Lift & Gains Charts:** These are used to show how much better the model is at identifying churners compared to a random guess. For example, "the top 10% of members ranked by the model's score contain 40% of all actual churners."

### 4. Data Dictionary & Lineage

The model is trained on an ABT that is largely inherited from the Actuarial Pricing Model's output.

| Column Name                     | Data Type     | Source System             | Description                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Patient_KEY`                   | `String`      | Member Database           | Unique identifier for the member.                                                                        |
| `age`                           | `Integer`     | Member Database           | Member's age.                                                                                            |
| `premium_increase_percentage`   | `Double`      | Pricing Model Output      | The percentage increase in the member's premium for the upcoming year. A key "price shock" feature.      |
| `out_of_pocket_ratio`           | `Double`      | Claims History            | The ratio of costs paid by the member (co-pays, deductibles) to the total cost of their care.            |
| `pre_auth_denial_rate`          | `Double`      | Pre-Authorization System  | The percentage of the member's pre-authorization requests that were denied. A measure of "clinical friction." |
| `manual_adjudication_rate`      | `Double`      | STP Model Output          | The percentage of the member's claims that were sent to `PEND` by the STP model. A measure of "delay friction." |
| `will_churn`                    | `Integer`     | Member Database           | **Target Variable:** 1 if the member did not renew their policy in the subsequent year, 0 otherwise.      |

**Data Lineage:**
1.  **Upstream ABT:** The process starts by loading the `abt_renewal_champion_featured.parquet` file, which is the final output of the Actuarial Pricing Model's feature engineering step.
2.  **Integration:** This ABT is then enriched with data from other systems, such as the `manual_adjudication_rate` from the STP model's logs.
3.  **Feature Engineering (`train.py`):** The final churn-specific features (like `premium_increase_percentage`) are calculated.

### 5. Feature Engineering & Logic

The features are designed to capture sources of "friction" that can lead to member dissatisfaction.

-   **`premium_increase_percentage` (Price Friction):**
    -   **Logic:** `(new_premium - old_premium) / old_premium`
    -   **Purpose:** The single most important predictor of churn. Large, unexpected price increases are a primary driver of attrition.

-   **`out_of_pocket_ratio` (Cost Friction):**
    -   **Logic:** `total_member_paid / total_claim_cost`
    -   **Purpose:** To measure the financial burden on the member. Even with insurance, high co-pays and deductibles can lead to dissatisfaction.

-   **`pre_auth_denial_rate` & `manual_adjudication_rate` (Service Friction):**
    -   **Purpose:** To quantify the administrative hassle a member experiences. Frequent denials or delays in payment create a poor customer experience.
    -   **Considerations:** This demonstrates the power of the Hub & Spoke architecture, where operational metrics from one model (STP) become predictive features for another (Churn).

---

### 6. Model vs. Rules-Based Logic

-   **Rules-Based Logic:** A business might use simple rules for retention, such as:
    -   `IF premium_increase > 20% THEN target_for_retention`.
    This is a one-dimensional approach that misses the complex interplay of factors that lead to churn.

-   **Machine Learning Model:** The LightGBM model can learn nuanced patterns, such as:
    -   A member might tolerate a 15% premium increase if their `out_of_pocket_ratio` is very low.
    -   However, a member who has experienced a 10% premium increase *and* has had two pre-authorizations denied might be a very high churn risk.
    The model's ability to capture these interactions makes it far more effective than simple rules.

---

### 7. Stakeholders & Downstream Consumers

-   **Marketing & Retention Teams:** The primary users of the model. They use the churn risk scores to build and target their retention campaigns.
-   **Customer Service Teams:** Can use the risk score to tailor their interactions with members. A high-risk member might be offered a more conciliatory tone or a special discount.
-   **Marketing Automation Platform:** The downstream system that ingests the churn scores and executes the targeted retention campaigns.

---

### 8. Risk Assessment & Mitigation

-   **Risk of Upstream Data Dependency:**
    -   **Risk:** The Churn Model is highly dependent on the outputs of the Pricing and STP models. If there is an error in one of these upstream models, it will propagate downstream and negatively impact the churn predictions.
    -   **Mitigation:** The entire MLOps pipeline must be structured as a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG). The Churn Model's training job should only run if all its upstream dependencies have completed successfully. Robust data quality checks on the incoming ABT are also essential.

-   **Risk of "Wasted" Retention Efforts:**
    -   **Risk:** If the model has low precision, the business may spend money trying to retain members who were never going to leave in the first place.
    -   **Mitigation:** The model's probability threshold can be tuned to optimize for a specific business goal (e.g., maximize the identification of churners in the top 5% of scores) using lift and gains charts.

### 9. Scenario Walkthroughs & Examples

**Scenario 1: High Churn Risk**
-   **Member Data:**
    -   `premium_increase_percentage`: 18%
    -   `pre_auth_denial_rate`: 0.5 (50% of requests denied)
    -   `manual_adjudication_rate`: 0.4 (40% of claims required manual review)
-   **Model Prediction:** The model sees a combination of high price shock and high service friction and predicts a high churn probability (e.g., 0.90).
-   **Outcome:** The member is placed at the top of the list for a proactive retention call from a senior customer service agent.

**Scenario 2: Low Churn Risk**
-   **Member Data:**
    -   `premium_increase_percentage`: 5%
    -   `pre_auth_denial_rate`: 0.0
    -   `manual_adjudication_rate`: 0.0
-   **Model Prediction:** Despite a small price increase, the member has had a smooth, frictionless experience. The model predicts a very low churn probability (e.g., 0.02).
-   **Outcome:** The member is not targeted for any specific retention action.

---

### 10. Operational & Implementation Details

-   **Pipeline Dependency:** The training pipeline for this model must be orchestrated to run only after the successful completion of the upstream model pipelines that generate its input data.
-   **Scoring:** The model is used to score the entire member base in a batch process prior to the renewal campaign season.
-   **Output:** The output is a list of members and their associated churn risk scores, which is then loaded into the marketing automation platform.
-   **Retraining Cadence:** The model is retrained annually, incorporating the churn results from the most recent renewal cycle.
